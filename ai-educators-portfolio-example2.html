Asset 01

Refined Lesson Plan

The lesson starts with my intended student behaviour. LLM is used to challenge the design and suggest ways to make the learning observable.

1

Working Effectively in a Team

40–45 minute life skills / employability lesson
Teacher-facing

Learning objective

Students will demonstrate three observable teamwork behaviours: active listening, constructive contribution, and building on another person's idea.

Success criteria

  • I listen without interrupting.
  • I contribute something relevant.
  • I acknowledge or build on another person's idea.

Lesson sequence

1 · Starter — 5 minutes

Ask students to think of a team that worked well and identify one behaviour that made the team effective. Collect behaviours rather than names.

2 · Make the invisible visible — 7 minutes

Introduce the three target behaviours: Listen, Contribute, Build. Model each one briefly with a volunteer.

3 · Team challenge — 15 minutes

In small groups, students complete a short planning challenge. Each student receives a different piece of information needed by the team. The task cannot be completed effectively unless students listen to one another and share information.

4 · Reflection — 8 minutes

Students identify one behaviour they demonstrated and one behaviour they want to improve.

5 · Exit check — 5 minutes

Students complete three short prompts about observable teamwork behaviour.

What I decided

  • The lesson should focus on behaviour, not vague personality traits.
  • Students need to experience teamwork, not just discuss it.
  • Assessment should be based on observable actions.
  • The activity must require information sharing so teamwork is genuinely needed.

Where AI supported the process

  • Suggested alternative team challenges.
  • Helped identify behaviours that could be observed during the activity.
  • Generated possible reflection questions.
  • Helped create differentiated versions without changing the core objective.
Important: AI does not decide whether a student is “good at teamwork.” The teacher observes specific behaviours against agreed criteria.
Asset 02

Student Activity & Assessment

The activity is deliberately designed so the teamwork behaviours are necessary to complete the task.

2

The Team Challenge

Students must combine information to complete the task
Student-facing

Your team has a problem to solve.

15 minutes

Your team is planning a one-day event for a group of students. Your team must agree on:

  1. The order of the activities.
  2. How much time to give each activity.
  3. What resources are needed.
  4. One reason for your final plan.
Important rule: Each team member receives different information. You need to listen to one another to get the complete picture.

Your three teamwork behaviours

1
LISTEN

Give attention when another person is speaking. Do not interrupt. Ask for clarification if needed.

2
CONTRIBUTE

Share relevant information, an idea, a question or a useful suggestion.

3
BUILD

Respond to another person's contribution by extending it, connecting it to something else, or asking a useful follow-up question.

Teacher observation rubric

Behaviour Beginning Demonstrated Consistent
Listen Frequently interrupts or misses information from others. Usually allows others to speak and responds to what they said. Consistently listens, checks understanding and responds appropriately.
Contribute Contribution is limited or unrelated to the task. Offers relevant information, ideas or questions. Makes useful contributions that help the group move forward.
Build Rarely responds to others' contributions. Acknowledges or develops another person's idea. Regularly connects, extends or improves ideas collaboratively.

Student reflection

  1. Which teamwork behaviour did you demonstrate?
  2. What did you do that showed this?
  3. Which behaviour will you deliberately practise next time?

Why this is assessable

The assessment does not attempt to measure personality. It looks for specific, observable behaviours during a defined activity.

Asset 03

Communication Draft

A short message that prepares students for the learning experience rather than simply announcing it.

3

Student pre-lesson message

Setting expectations for the teamwork activity
Communication

Tomorrow's lesson: Teamwork

Tomorrow we'll be doing a short team challenge. The goal isn't simply to finish the task — we'll also be practising what effective teamwork actually looks like.

During the activity, pay particular attention to three behaviours:

  • Listen — give people space to speak and pay attention to their information.
  • Contribute — share useful information, ideas or questions.
  • Build — respond to someone else's idea and help develop it.

You don't need to be the person who talks the most. Good teamwork is about helping the group make progress together.

Come ready to participate, listen and try something deliberately different from your usual group-work habits.

My judgement

I wanted the message to avoid presenting teamwork as a personality trait. Students should understand that these are behaviours they can practise and improve.

AI support

AI helped me create alternative versions with different tones and lengths, then I chose and edited the version that sounded appropriate for my students.

Asset 05

My Repeatable AI Routine

This is the asset I would carry forward from the course: a repeatable process rather than a single successful prompt.

5

Human-Led Lesson Design Loop

A reusable workflow for future lessons
Repeatable
STEP 01

Define

I decide what students actually need to learn or practise.

STEP 02

Describe

I give the LLM my context, constraints and rough idea.

STEP 03

Challenge

I ask the LLM to find gaps, misconceptions and alternatives.

STEP 04

Build

I use the useful suggestions to create activities and resources.

STEP 05

Decide

I review, edit, reject or keep the output before using it.

Example of a reusable prompt

I am an educator planning a lesson on [topic / skill] for [learner age / level].
Here is my initial idea and context:
[Paste my rough lesson idea, learning objective, classroom context, learner needs, constraints, and anything I already know I want to do.]
Help me improve this without replacing my professional judgment or completely changing my teaching approach.
First, analyse my idea. Identify:
  • what is already working well;
  • possible gaps or weaknesses;
  • likely points of confusion or difficulty for learners;
  • opportunities to make the learning objective clearer;
  • opportunities for meaningful differentiation;
  • questions I could ask to check understanding.

Then suggest improvements while preserving the parts of my approach that are intentional and useful. Give me alternatives where there is more than one reasonable approach rather than assuming there is one perfect solution.
Do not assume your suggestions are automatically appropriate. Explain the reasoning behind important choices and flag anything that I should verify based on my learners, curriculum, classroom context, or professional knowledge.
Once we have refined the lesson, help me create:
  1. a clear lesson sequence;
  2. a student activity aligned to the learning objective;
  3. support or scaffolding for learners who need it;
  4. an extension or challenge for learners who are ready;
  5. observable assessment criteria;
  6. teacher questioning prompts;
  7. any simple supporting resources that would make the lesson easier to teach.

Keep the materials practical and realistic for an actual classroom. Avoid adding activities simply for the sake of using AI.
Finally, give me a short quality-control checklist so I can review the finished materials myself before using them with students.
Reflection

What I take away from the learning

The value of the course is not simply in producing five finished assets. It is in developing a way of working with AI that can be adapted, questioned and improved through real teaching practice.

What changed in my practice

AI became part of the process

The biggest value of using LLMs was not simply generating teaching materials. It gave me another way to examine my own thinking, test an idea and quickly explore alternatives.

  • I can start with an idea I already have
  • I can ask AI to challenge or expand my thinking
  • I can explore multiple approaches quickly
  • I can turn useful ideas into practical resources
  • I still make the final teaching decisions
Human expertise remains central
What I would do next

Iterate from real classroom experience

The first version does not need to be the final version. I can use what happens in practice to make both my prompts and my teaching materials more useful over time.

  • Give LLM better context about my learners
  • Refine prompts based on what actually worked
  • Compare generated options before choosing
  • Keep the resources that prove useful
  • Build a library of repeatable workflows
Improvement happens through iteration
My takeaway:

We do not need to reproduce any particular AI workflow exactly the way a single course or learning material demonstrates. The more useful outcome is understanding the principles well enough to adapt them to the way we already work as an educator/trainer/learning designer.

The mindset

Start → test → reflect → improve

The portfolio is therefore not a finished endpoint. It is a starting point for a workflow that can become more effective with each iteration.

01 · START

Bring my expertise

Begin with what I already know about the subject, learners and classroom context.

02 · TEST

Work with AI

Use LLMs to question, develop and explore possible approaches.

03 · REFLECT

Look at what happened

Consider what was useful, what was not, and what students actually responded to.

04 · ITERATE

Make the next version better

Improve the prompt, activity, resource or workflow rather than treating the first version as final.
